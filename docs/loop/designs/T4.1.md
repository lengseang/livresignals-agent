# T4.1 · Design: fill model for the paper engine

Status: design for T4.2. **Paper-only.** The model decides *whether* and *at what price* a simulated order
fills. It never talks to an exchange.

Inputs: `docs/research/fees-and-breakeven.md` (the cost floor), `docs/research/spreads.md` (measured spreads, L1 depth
and latency from a 7.56 h sample), `docs/research/strategy-audit.md` #7 (maker entries need non-fill risk), and the
recorder format in `docs/loop/designs/T2.1.md`.

## Problem

Paper results are only as honest as their fills. The two classic ways a paper engine lies:
1. **Free taker fills**: filling at mid or last trade, with no fee and no spread. For small targets that turns a
   losing strategy into a winning one (a 0.3/0.3% scalp needs 83% instead of 50%; see `fees-and-breakeven.md`).
2. **Free maker fills**: counting a limit order as filled whenever price *touches* it. Real resting orders sit
   in a queue. They fill mostly when price moves *through* them, which is exactly when the trade is going wrong
   (adverse selection), and they don't fill when price runs away in the direction you wanted (missed winners).

## Design

One module, `src/trading/paper/fills.py` (new, T4.2). It imports nothing from `trading.exchange`, and an import-graph test
proves it. It has pure functions over a small config and a book or bar snapshot.

### Configuration (all explicit, all in the paper result header)

| Parameter | Default | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `fee_taker`, `fee_maker` | 0.0010 each | VIP 0 default; the real rate is UNKNOWN, so it's overridable per run and printed on every report |
| `spread_bp[symbol]` | the median column of `spreads.md` | measured; used **only in replay without a book** (see Replay) |
| `latency_order_s` | 0.38 | the measured one-way feed latency, reused as the order leg (a scenario). Sweep {0, 0.38, 0.75, 1.5} in reports |
| `maker_rule` | `trade_through` | conservative. `touch` exists **only** as a labelled optimistic sensitivity run |
| `maker_timeout_s` | set per strategy | a resting order is cancelled after this; the cancel is a recorded non-fill |
| `beyond_l1` | `cancel_rest` | the part larger than L1 is not filled and is flagged. `penalty` is a labelled pessimistic scenario |

### Taker (market / marketable) orders, with a live or recorded book

1. The strategy decides at time `t`, using data it received by `t`. Receive-stamped data already includes the
   feed leg.
2. The order "arrives" at `t + latency_order_s`. Take the **latest book at or before the arrival time**, not the
   book at `t`. Price moves during latency are therefore charged.
3. Buy at that book's **ask**, sell at its **bid**. That's the full half-spread from mid, charged on every side.
4. Size: fill `min(qty, L1 qty on that side)` at the best price. The remainder follows `beyond_l1`:
   - `cancel_rest` (default): not filled. The fill carries `flags={"exceeds_top_level"}` and the strategy sees a
     **partial fill**. Nothing is priced beyond L1, because nothing beyond L1 is known.
   - `penalty` (scenario): the remainder is charged at best ± the symbol's **p99** spread. It's labelled as a scenario in
     the result, never as a measured cost.
5. Fee: `fee_taker × notional`, deducted in quote currency.

At the sizes this project trades, $5 fits L1 at ≥ 99.4% of real trades. $50 doesn't reliably fit on AVAX,
LINK, SUI, BTC, ETH or BNB (13–25% of real trades exceed L1, an upper bound; see `spreads.md`). So partial fills are a real
path, not an edge case.

### Maker (resting limit) orders

1. The order rests from `t + latency_order_s` at limit price `L`. If it's marketable on arrival (a buy with `L ≥ ask`),
   it's treated as a **taker** order at the ask, as the exchange would.
2. **Fill rule `trade_through` (default).** A resting buy at `L` fills only when a real trade prints **strictly below `L`**,
   and a sell only when a trade prints strictly above `L`. A trade *at* `L` doesn't count: the queue ahead of us at `L`
   (unknown, often the whole displayed L1 quantity) may absorb it. A trade through `L` means the whole queue at `L`
   was consumed, and us with it.
3. **Price**: `L` exactly. No price improvement.
4. **Size**: the full order when it's traded through (the sizes here are far below the volume that clears a level). If
   T4.3 finds sizes near the traded volume, fill `min(qty, volume traded through L)` instead.
5. Fee: `fee_maker × notional`. At VIP 0 that's the same as taker, so **a maker entry saves only the half-spread**.
   On BTC and ETH that's 0.0006 and 0.019 bp per side, on DOT 4.2 bp.
6. **Non-fills are recorded events, not silence.** Every resting order ends as `filled`, `cancelled_timeout` or
   `cancelled_by_strategy`, and the report prints the **fill rate** next to the P&L. For a missed order, the report also
   records what the trade *would* have made, so the cost of missed winners is visible.
7. **Adverse selection note.** Under `trade_through`, a buy fills only after price has traded below it. That's the
   moment the short-term drift is against us. Fills are therefore biased toward losers and non-fills toward
   winners. The engine must mark a maker position to market from the *actual* post-fill path. It must never assume a
   fill at `L` followed by a reversion. T4.2 reports the **markout** of every maker fill (mid − fill price at
   +1 s, +10 s and +60 s, signed by side). A negative average markout is adverse selection, measured rather than assumed.
8. `touch` (sensitivity only): a fill when a trade prints *at* `L`. Its result is labelled OPTIMISTIC. The gap between
   the `touch` and `trade_through` P&L is the queue-position uncertainty. If a strategy is profitable only under
   `touch`, it's not profitable.

**Consequence for strategy choice.** Because VIP 0 maker fees equal taker fees, maker entries on BTC, ETH, BNB and XRP buy
almost nothing (≤ 0.33 bp per side saved) and add non-fill and adverse-selection risk. The maker variants from
`strategy-audit.md` #7 make sense to test **only on coarse-tick symbols** (DOT, ATOM, ARB, ADA, NEAR: 1.0–4.2 bp per side
saved), or once a real maker rebate or discount is known.

### Replay without a book (klines only: T2.5/T2.6 history, 1s and 1m)

History has OHLCV bars and no bid/ask. Rules:

1. **Decision on bar close, execution in the next bar.** A signal computed on bar `k` executes at bar `k+1`'s **open**.
   With 1s bars the ~0.75 s two-leg latency (feed plus order, both scenario-level) falls inside one bar. With 1m bars
   latency is dominated by the bar anyway.
2. **Taker price** = next open × (1 ± `spread_bp[symbol]` / 2 / 1e4), plus the fee. The spread is the **measured median**
   from `spreads.md`, a documented approximation, since there is no book.
3. **Maker (limit) fill**: buy at `L` fills in a bar only if `low < L` strictly (trade-through), and sell only if `high > L`.
   Price `L`. A bar with `low == L` is a non-fill.
4. **Stops** are taker orders: triggered when `low ≤ stop` (for a long). The fill is `min(stop, open)` if the bar gapped
   through, minus the half-spread and the fee. **Take-profits** placed as limits use rule 3.
5. **Same-bar ambiguity**: when a bar touches both the stop and the take-profit, assume the **stop first**. OHLC can't
   show the order, and the pessimistic choice is the one that can't flatter.
6. **Depth**: bars have none. Replay orders are capped at $5 notional per fill by default. Above that, the replay
   result gets the label `depth unmodelled`, because `spreads.md` shows $50 exceeding L1 in a noticeable share of cases.

**Why the median spread flatters or penalises replay results:**
- *Flatters*: signals cluster in fast markets, which is exactly when spreads widen. The median ignores the tail (ATOM
  p99 is 11.3 bp against a 5.6 bp median; ARB p90 is 8.8 bp against 4.5 bp). The sample is one quiet evening, and missing
  L1 depth plus latency drift is ignored. The net direction for signal-driven strategies is **flattering**. To
  compensate, every replay report also runs with the **p90** spread, and a result that doesn't survive p90 is not reported as
  robust.
- *Penalises or distorts over long history*: the spread is about one tick, so in bp it's `tick / price`. When a coin traded
  at a higher price than today (DOT and ATOM in 2021, for example), the real bp spread was *smaller*, and today's bp
  penalises that period. When it traded lower, today's bp flatters it. Historical tick sizes have also changed and are
  **UNKNOWN** here. So bp-from-today is wrong in both directions across years. T4.2 stores the spread in
  **ticks and bp** both, and the replay report states which it used. Per-period correction is out of scope until
  historical tick sizes are sourced.

### Replay with the recorded book (T2.2 ticks)

Same as the live rules: taker from the latest book record at `t + latency`, maker from real trades through `L`.
Caveat: bookTicker records are thinned (price changes plus about 1/s), so L1 *quantity* between records is approximate. Prices
are exact.

## Break-even, re-run with measured spreads

`research/08-spread-stats.py --breakeven` reuses `research/07-breakeven.py`'s formula with slip per side = half the
measured median spread (full tables in `spreads.md`). At **VIP 0 (0.100%/side)**:

| TP / SL | slip 0 | BTC (0.0012 bp) | median symbol (0.95 bp) | DOT (8.37 bp) | ARB p90 (8.83 bp) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.20 / 0.20% | IMPOSS. | IMPOSS. | IMPOSS. | IMPOSS. | IMPOSS. |
| 0.30 / 0.30% | 83.3% | 83.3% | 84.9% | 97.3% | 98.0% |
| 0.50 / 0.50% | 70.0% | 70.0% | 71.0% | 78.4% | 78.8% |
| 0.50 / 0.25% | 60.0% | 60.0% | 61.3% | 71.2% | 71.8% |
| 1.00 / 1.00% | 60.0% | 60.0% | 60.5% | 64.2% | 64.4% |
| 2.00 / 1.00% | 40.0% | 40.0% | 40.3% | 42.8% | 42.9% |

Highlights:
- On the majors the **fee is the entire cost**. The measured spread moves p* by < 0.1 pp on BTC and 1.6 pp on the median
  symbol at 0.3/0.3%.
- On DOT/ARB, spread is a second fee: a 0.3/0.3% scalp needs 97–98%, and a 0.5/0.5% one about 78%.
- The earlier "slip 2 bp / 5 bp" columns in `fees-and-breakeven.md` overstated the majors' cost and roughly matched the
  coarse-tick coins. These are **lower bounds**: slippage beyond L1, latency drift and non-fills are extra.

## Options considered

| Option | Why not (or why) |
|---|---|
| Fill at mid / last trade | free spread; the known lie #1 |
| Maker fill on touch | ignores the queue; the known lie #2. Kept only as a labelled sensitivity run |
| Probabilistic queue model (fill after the volume traded at `L` exceeds the displayed L1 at placement) | better in principle, but it needs quantity-only updates, which the recorder drops, and its parameters would be guesses. Revisit if the recorder keeps them for one symbol |
| Price the remainder beyond L1 from a slippage curve | no depth recorded, so the curve would be invented. Rejected until `@depth` data exists |
| Fixed bp slip for all symbols (the old 2/5 bp) | measured spreads differ by ~7000× between BTC and DOT; one number is wrong for everyone |

## Pre-mortem: it's 3 months later and this failed. Why?

- **The 7.6 h sample was unrepresentative.** The first volatile day doubles the spreads and paper P&L diverges from any
  real fill. Mitigation: the recorder keeps running. `spreads.md` is re-run weekly, and replay reports always include the
  p90 run. Reverse if the weekly median moves by > 1 tick on any symbol.
- **The fee was wrong.** The account turns out to have a BNB discount (fees overstated) or no discount (the repo's
  0.072% understated). Mitigation: the fee is a parameter printed on every report. When `fee-audit.json` arrives,
  every paper result is re-run, not patched.
- **Maker strategies looked good under `trade_through` and still lost live.** The rule is conservative on the queue but
  says nothing about how fast *we* can cancel. A slow cancel means stale quotes get picked off. Mitigation: the markout report,
  and the order latency sweep also applied to cancels.
- **A strategy learned the fill model.** Many variants tuned on the same cheap `touch` or median assumptions. Mitigation:
  `touch` never feeds the gate (T3.3). Only `trade_through` with p90 spread results count as trials.
- **Latency was wrong by 2×.** The ±334 ms error is as large as the value. Mitigation: the latency sweep is mandatory in
  T4.3 reports. A strategy whose edge disappears between 0.38 s and 0.75 s is labelled latency-fragile.
- **Clock skew leaked into replay.** Mixing `ts_recv` (local, +5.3 s) with exchange timestamps would give fills from the
  "future". Mitigation: the engine uses one clock per replay (receive clock for recorded ticks, exchange clock for klines)
  and a test asserts that no fill uses a book record received after the fill time.

## Test plan (T4.2)

1. **Taker price**: a buy fills at the ask and a sell at the bid of the book at `t + latency`, not at `t`. The fixture has the ask
   moving during the latency window.
2. **Fee**: the fee equals `fee_taker × notional` and is configurable. The default 0.001 is printed in the result header.
3. **Partial fill**: qty > L1 → filled = L1 qty at best, remainder not filled, `exceeds_top_level` flagged
   (`cancel_rest`). `penalty` prices the remainder at the p99 spread and labels it a scenario.
4. **Maker trade-through**: a trade at `L` doesn't fill a resting buy, and a trade at `L − 1 tick` does, at price `L`.
   Mirror test for sells.
5. **Maker marketable on arrival** is treated as taker.
6. **Non-fill**: an order that is never traded through ends `cancelled_timeout` at `maker_timeout_s`, and it's counted in the
   fill rate.
7. **Markout**: for a hand-made path after a fill, the +1/+10/+60 s markouts equal hand-computed values.
8. **Replay bars**: next-open execution; `low == L` is a non-fill and `low < L` a fill; a bar touching both SL and TP books the SL;
   a gap through the stop fills at the open.
9. **Clock discipline**: no fill uses a book record with `ts_recv` after the order's arrival time (property test over a
   recorded fixture).
10. **Import guard**: `trading.paper` imports nothing from `trading.exchange`, ccxt, or any `BLOCKED_MODULES` entry.
11. **Labels**: every fill record carries `paper: true` and the fill-model parameters it was produced with.
